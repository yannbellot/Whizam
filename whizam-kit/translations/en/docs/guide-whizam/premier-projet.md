# Creating your first project

Choose a name and identify your working copy. For an existing project, use [Resuming and handing off](reprendre-transmettre.md).

> Fictional example: “I work with Codex. I want to initialize a project called Atelier. Prepare an initialization proposal.”

The assistant examines existing files and presents the preparation. Approving that proposal authorizes covered operations; it does not mean accepting an application that has yet to be designed.

## Understand the four sequences

1. **Initialization**: check and prepare the environment and tracking documents. The project is ready for scoping; no business features are inferred from the kit.
2. **Scoping**: define an observable first version, content, exclusions and success criteria. You decide on the proposed scope.
3. **Design and development**: implement authorized pages, interactions and components, then verify the result. Temporary content and simulations must be identified.
4. **Review**: explore the delivered version and components, provide feedback and decide on acceptance when the criteria allow it.

These milestones do not require renewed approval for every already authorized operation. A missing material decision may require a further exchange.

## Know what is ready

Completed initialization does not mean the application has been built. At review, expect verified access to your application, relevant Storybook components and documentation. A generic page or kit example does not demonstrate completion of your request.

Prepare your intent with [Scoping a first version](cadrer-version.md). The [startup workflow](../product/project-start.md) defines each sequence.

## Scoping retained across conversations

The assistant collects only missing information: name, objective/audience, first version, content, visual references, devices, PWA, offline access, application languages then documentation/Storybook languages. After selecting an option promising material, reply in conversation with text, files, links or paths. Optional details can avoid this follow-up.

A demonstration proposes the first six answers and all three devices, then resumes at PWA, offline and language choices after agreement. Development awaits approval of the complete synthesis. The brief/checkpoint retain answers; new conversations do not repeat the questionnaire.
